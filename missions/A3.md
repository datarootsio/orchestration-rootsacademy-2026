# A3 — Transform, load, prove it

**20 minutes.**

## Where we are

The real 2026-03-03 delivery has arrived. Finance wants it in the warehouse, and
they want to know it is right.

## What this mission is about

Building a real dependency chain, deciding where validation belongs, and
discovering that re-running a pipeline is a design problem rather than a button.

## Your task

1. **Extend the DAG:** clean → aggregate → load into your team's
   `daily_revenue` table. Three tasks are stubbed for you: `transform`,
   `load_warehouse`, `validate_load`.

2. **Keep validation as its own task.** `validate.py` is provided. Resist the
   urge to fold it into the transform — and when you have finished, work out why
   the brief asked you not to.

3. **Run the whole DAG twice.** The second run must not change the numbers.

4. **Wire the dependencies.** Including one that carries no data at all. Passing
   a value from one task to the next expresses "runs after" *and* "hands data
   to". Sometimes you only want the first.

## What success looks like

Every task green, one row per store in `daily_revenue` for 2026-03-03, and a
second full run leaving the row count and revenue totals unchanged.

```
roots verify a3
```

reports `warehouse_loaded` and `validation_passed`.

`validation_passed` requires **a distinct validation step that ran and
succeeded**. Correct data in Postgres is not sufficient. That is deliberate: the
shape of the solution is part of the answer, and this milestone is what enforces
it.

## Three things that will bite you

- **`PostgresOperator` does not exist in Airflow 3.** Every AI assistant will
  suggest it. Use `SQLExecuteQueryOperator` from
  `airflow.providers.common.sql.operators.sql` — and note that its `schema`
  argument is now called `database`. `ensure_warehouse_table` already shows you
  the correct form.
- **A plain `INSERT` doubles every figure on the second run**, and nothing looks
  broken. Look at the table's primary key.
- **Do not pass the dataset through XCom.** It may even work at this size, which
  makes it worse, not better.

## Where the layers are

- **Orchestration:** task order, the dependency chain, and the decision that
  validation is its own task.
- **Processing:** `clean.py`, `revenue.py`, `load.py`, `validate.py` — all
  provided, all sealed.
- **Boundary:** how data moves between tasks. Paths on disk, not XCom payloads.

## Think about this before the debrief

**Where did you put the validation, and why not inside the transform?**

Then: *it is 03:00 and this pipeline just failed. Which failure message would you
rather be paged with — "transform failed" or "validation failed: store RM-0233
missing"?*
