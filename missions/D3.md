# D3 — Selective recovery

**20 minutes.**

## Where we are

Finance is back with something new.

`daily_revenue` has been including store **`T-999`** — SupplyHub's internal test
store, which posts synthetic transactions. It has been in every figure all day,
including everything your Airflow pipeline produced this morning.

Nobody noticed.

## What this mission is about

Reasoning about what a corrected transformation rule actually invalidates — and
discovering that rebuilding everything is how a two-minute recovery becomes a
forty-minute one.

## Your task

1. **Fix the cleaning rule in your `clean_sales` asset** so that stores absent
   from `cfg.expected_stores` are excluded.

   `clean.clean_sales()` tells you which *expected* stores are missing. It
   deliberately does not drop *unexpected* ones — deciding which stores count as
   Rootsmarkt revenue is your business rule, not the source parser's. So the fix
   goes in your asset. The `rootsmarkt_processing` package stays sealed.

2. **Decide which assets must be re-materialized, and which must not.**

3. **Materialize exactly that set.** No more.

4. **Submit your justification:**

   ```
   roots submit d3 --rebuilt <asset,asset> --not-rebuilt <asset> --why "<your reasoning>"
   ```

## What success looks like

Revenue drops by `T-999`'s synthetic contribution and stays inside your plausible
bounds, so the D2 check remains green.

```
roots submit d3 --rebuilt … --not-rebuilt … --why "…"
roots status
```

D3 has no `roots verify` — both its milestones are judged elsewhere. Submitting
is what triggers them, and `roots status` shows the result.

`no_unnecessary_refetch` is judged on what SupplyHub actually saw. Clicking
**Materialize all** re-runs `raw_delivery`, re-downloads the file, and fails this
milestone.

`rebuild_justified` is the evidence submission.

## The trap

*Materialize all* is the single most likely action in this mission, and it is now
a detectable failure rather than merely an inefficiency.

Ask yourself what actually changed.

## Do not do this

Filtering against a hardcoded `["T-999"]`. It works today and breaks the next
time SupplyHub invents a test store. Use `cfg.expected_stores`.

## Where the layers are

- **Orchestration:** choosing the materialization set. That is the entire mission.
- **Processing:** the one-line filter — but note *where* it lives, and why it is
  in your asset rather than in the sealed package.
- **Boundary:** recognising that a logic change invalidates derived datasets but
  not acquired ones. This is the data-driven versus execution-driven dependency
  distinction, made concrete.
