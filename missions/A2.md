# A2 — The delivery lies

**20 minutes.** This is one of the two missions that matter most today.

## Where we are

It is the next morning in story time. You ask SupplyHub for **2026-03-03**.

The task goes green.

Finance says the numbers haven't moved.

## What this mission is about

Two things, and the second is harder than the first.

An external system returning success is not evidence that it did what you asked.
That is the diagnosis.

And then: **"retry" is a decision that requires a hypothesis about why something
failed** — not a reflex. Adding `retries=3` is the single most common response to
a flaky external system, and sometimes it is exactly right. Work out whether it
is right *here*, and be able to say why.

## Your task

1. **Run the pipeline for business date 2026-03-03.** Trigger the DAG with
   `{"business_date": "2026-03-03"}` in the config.

2. **Work out what you actually received, and why that is a problem.**
   Everything you need is in the task logs and the file on disk. The CSV is
   valid — do not go hunting for a parsing bug.

3. **Submit your evidence.** All four values come from what SupplyHub actually
   served *your* team:

   ```
   roots submit a2 --received-date <date> --expected-date <date> --delivery-id <id> --row-count <n>
   ```

4. **Make the DAG refuse to proceed** on a delivery that is not the one you asked
   for. There is a task waiting for this: `assert_delivery_is_current`.

5. **Decide what the pipeline should do about it** — retry, wait, fail, or skip —
   and be ready to defend your choice in the debrief.

## What success looks like

The fetch returns **HTTP 200**. After your fix, the DAG stops *deliberately and
legibly*: a log line naming the mismatch, not a stack trace. Someone reading it
at 03:00 should understand it without opening the code.

A2 has **two** milestones, earned two different ways:

| Milestone | How | Command |
|---|---|---|
| `stale_delivery_detected` | your evidence, checked against what SupplyHub actually served you | `roots submit a2 …` (step 3) |
| `stale_delivery_blocked` | your guard, observed in Airflow's run history | `roots verify a2` |

```
roots verify a2
```

**Your chosen response is not graded.** `stale_delivery_blocked` asks only
whether a guard exists — whether it passed a delivery it should have passed and
stopped one it should have stopped. Retry, wait, fail and skip all satisfy it
equally. There is more than one defensible answer here and that is deliberate:
the reasoning is the point, not the verdict.

It does mean you have to run the pipeline **twice** — once on a date SupplyHub
can serve and once on 2026-03-03, which it cannot. A guard that has only ever
blocked, or only ever passed, has not been shown to discriminate.

## Where the layers are

This is the sharpest boundary in the Airflow half, and it is worth sitting with.

The same three lines are **processing** when they compute whether the dates
match, and **orchestration** when that comparison decides the run stops.

- **Orchestration:** where the guard sits (before any downstream work), and what
  it does to the run.
- **Processing:** the comparison itself.

## Think about this before the debrief

**Who chose to retry, who chose to fail, and who chose to wait?**

Then: *what would have to be true about SupplyHub for each of the other three
answers to be the right one?*
